Last week we finished a stepping-stone path that started as a bare dirt track. It ran from the house down to the shed, and every time it rained it was mud. These are our own photos from the job, in the order the work happened.

## Before: a mud track

![Bare dirt track with pallets of pavers by the shed, before we started](/work/path-before-01.jpg)

The track was bare dirt, with pallets of pavers waiting by the shed. There was no path to speak of — just the line everyone walked, getting muddier through the wet season.

## During: dug out, levelled, compacted

![Dug-out bare path with a yellow wheelbarrow and the house behind, no pavers yet](/work/path-during-dug.jpg)

First we dug the track out. Then we levelled it and compacted the base with the blue compactor, so the stones would sit on something firm.

![Blue compactor on the dug path with a wheelbarrow full of soil](/work/path-during-compactor.jpg)

Most of the work is under the stones, and nobody photographs that part — but it is what keeps the path from moving. Skip the prep and the path shifts. We do not skip the prep.

## During: laid by hand

![Josh kneeling to place a paver on the new stone path](/work/path-during-kneeling.jpg)

Then we laid each paver by hand, kneeling, lifting, and levelling one at a time. No crew, no shortcuts — that is who shows up when you book us.

![Josh lifting a paver into place, with the finished path behind](/work/path-during-lifting.jpg)

## After: a proper path

![Finished stepping-stone path with a dog standing on the pavers](/work/path-after-dog.jpg)

Same yard, finished. Stone all the way from the house to the shed, and the dog approved it before we had even packed up.
